Design for people

Universal design creates products and environments that are useful, understandable, and comfortable for the widest possible range of people.

Equitable use

Provide the same experience whenever possible, or an equivalent experience that preserves privacy, security, and dignity. Avoid making accessibility a separate or secondary option.

Flexible use

Support left- and right-handed use, different navigation preferences, changing ability, and a range of pace and precision.

Perceptible information

Use clear language, strong contrast, text alternatives, visible labels, and ways to perceive essential information without relying on only one sense.

A practical process

  1. Include: involve people with disabilities throughout the project.
  2. Check: test keyboard operation, zoom, headings, links, forms, and contrast.
  3. Listen: test with assistive technologies and people using the result.

Quick checklist

  • Page language is set
  • Headings follow a useful hierarchy
  • Links describe their destination
  • Keyboard focus remains visible
  • Text and interface elements have sufficient contrast
